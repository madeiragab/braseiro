# The rulebooks

## Where

**The short way: drag the files onto the Braseiro window.** They go into the open
campaign, PDFs are converted on the spot, indexed, and it replies in the chat
telling you what went in and what did not. The **📚 adicionar livros do sistema**
button in the panel does the same through an Explorer dialog.

By hand, it is inside the campaign folder, in `Documents/Braseiro/<your campaign>/`.
As many subfolders as you want:

```
Documents/Braseiro/minha-campanha/livros/
├── dnd5e/
│   ├── combat.md
│   ├── spells.md
│   └── conditions.md
├── savage-worlds/
│   └── skills.md
└── my-setting/
    └── pantheon/
        └── gods.md
```

Accepted formats: **`.pdf`**, `.md`, `.markdown` and `.txt`.

## PDF

Drop the `.pdf` in the folder. The first time it becomes a `.pdf.txt` beside it,
and from then on it's text like any other. The conversion only reruns if you
replace the PDF.

A big book takes a while — and the conversion would stall your first turn in
silence. For those, run **`CONVERTER-PDF.bat`** first: it converts everything
with visible progress.

The `.pdf.txt` is an ordinary file. **It's well worth opening and cleaning up**:
delete the table of contents and the credits page, fix where a section break
landed badly, and above all add `chaves:` lines under the headings.

### What works and what doesn't

| PDF | result |
|---|---|
| generated by a text editor, LaTeX, InDesign | ✅ extracts |
| compressed with FlateDecode (most of them) | ✅ extracts |
| **scanned** (the page is a photo) | ❌ no text underneath; only OCR would help |
| font with a private encoding and no `ToUnicode` map | ❌ would come out garbled |

When it can't, a `.pdf.aviso` appears beside the file explaining why, and
**nothing from that file is indexed**. That's deliberate: half a book of garbled
characters hurts a campaign more than no book at all. The **livros** tab in the
panel lists both cases.

For the ones that fail: in your PDF reader use "Save as" → text, or copy and
paste just the chapter you care about. That's usually better anyway — RPG books
are full of art, indexes and tables that turn into noise.

### How the extraction finds headings

PDFs have no `##`. So the converter promotes to a heading any short line (up to
60 characters) that doesn't end in punctuation and is either ALL CAPS or isolated
by a blank line — which is how RPG book chapters normally appear.

When no heading is detected in a chunk, the keywords are taken from the chunk's
**own text** (its most repeated words). Without that, untitled text would be
unreachable, because only a strong term fires a section.

## How to cut

`#` headings mark the sections. Each section becomes its own indexed entry.

```markdown
# Combat

## Initiative
chaves: initiative, order, surprise, ambush

Roll 1d20 + Dexterity, highest first...

## Attack
chaves: attack, attacking, hit, sword, weapon, blow, strike

1d20 + modifier against the target's AC...

## Dying
chaves: dying, death, unconscious, down, bleeding, stabilize

At 0 hit points the character falls...
```

> The keyword line must literally be `chaves:` — that's the word the parser looks
> for. Everything else can be in any language.

A file with **no headings at all** works too: it's sliced on its own into ~1200
character chunks, cutting at line ends. Good for pasting a raw chapter and
sorting it out later.

A section **larger than 1200 characters** is sliced the same way, and the pieces
get numbered: `Attack`, `Attack (2)`, `Attack (3)`.

## `chaves:` is what makes it work

This is the part that decides whether the system is useful or not.

The heading alone isn't enough. **Nobody types "combat" while playing.** You write:

> *I draw my sword and charge the orc*

Not one word in that sentence is "combat". Without `chaves:`, the rule is never found.

```markdown
## Combat
chaves: combat, attack, attacking, damage, wounded, sword, weapon, blow,
        fight, brawl, kill, enemy, orc, axe, shield
```

Rule of thumb: **write the list thinking about what you'd say at the table**, not
what's printed in the book. Be generous — one extra key costs almost nothing, one
missing key costs the whole rule.

### How matching works

Terms are normalized (accent-free, lowercase) and **truncated at 6 characters**.
That handles plurals and conjugation with no effort:

| you wrote | becomes | matches |
|---|---|---|
| `attacking` | `attack` | attack, attacks, attacking |
| `disarm` | `disarm` | disarm, disarms, disarmed |
| `spell` | `spell` | spell, spells |

Terms shorter than 4 characters are dropped, and so are stop words
(`the`, `and`, `for`, `of`, `table`, `rule`…).

### Strong and weak

| source | weight | fires on its own? |
|---|---|---|
| section heading | strong (×3) | ✅ |
| `chaves:` line | strong (×3) | ✅ |
| file and folder names | weak (×1) | ❌ |
| chunk text, when there's no heading | strong (×3) | ✅ |

The file path counts for little **on purpose**. If it counted the same, a file
named `combat.md` would let the word "combat" pull every section in it at once
and flood the context.

## Budget

Per turn, at most:

- **4 sections**
- **2600 characters** (~700 tokens)

Anything beyond that is dropped, starting with the lowest-scoring sections. Which
is why it pays to cut into small, specific sections: three 400-character sections
fit better and are more useful than one of 1200.

## Checking

The **livros** tab in the right-hand panel shows the index: how many sections,
from which files, with what headings — plus which PDFs converted and which
didn't. If a section you expected isn't listed, the cut didn't take —
probably the heading doesn't start with `#`.

Saved a new file? It enters the index on the next turn. Nothing to restart: the
index rebuilds itself when it notices a file's modification date changed.

## About copyright

What you put in your campaign's `livros/` folder stays on your computer, is read
only by your own machine, and goes nowhere. This repository distributes no rulebook — the example
in `livros/exemplo-d20/` is original text written to demonstrate the format.
